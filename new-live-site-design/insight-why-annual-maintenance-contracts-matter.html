Why an Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) protects your investment

The day the installer leaves, your systems start to age. How preventive maintenance, response-time SLAs, monitoring and spares protect what you have already paid for.

An ELV installation is a capital investment that is supposed to run for a decade or more — cabling, network, cameras, access control, AV, fire detection and the software that ties them together. Yet the day the contractor leaves is the day that investment starts to age. Firmware falls behind, batteries and drives wear out, small faults accumulate unseen, and standards move on. An Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) is how you protect the value you have already paid for, instead of waiting for something critical to fail.

Preventive versus reactive maintenance

The core choice is whether you fix things before or after they break. Reactive maintenance waits for failure, which means unplanned downtime, emergency call-out costs, and problems discovered at the worst possible moment — a camera found dead only when footage is needed, or a fire panel fault surfacing during an inspection. Preventive maintenance schedules regular inspection, testing, cleaning, firmware updates and battery replacement so faults are caught and corrected while they are still minor. For life-safety systems governed by EN 54, SBC 801 and Civil Defense requirements, scheduled testing is not optional — it is part of staying compliant.

What a good AMC includes

A meaningful AMC is more than an annual visit. It defines response-time SLAs, so you know how quickly help arrives when something does go wrong, with tighter targets for critical systems. It can include proactive remote monitoring that flags a failing device or a network fault before it becomes an outage. It secures access to spares, so a replacement is on hand rather than weeks away on order. And for demanding sites it extends to on-site staffing — technicians embedded in the facility who know the systems intimately and keep them running day to day. Each of these is a lever you set to match how critical the systems are.

Total cost of ownership

Maintenance reads as a cost until you compare it with the alternative. Unplanned downtime, emergency repairs, premature replacement of neglected equipment, and the operational impact of a system being unavailable usually dwarf the price of a planned contract. Well-maintained systems last longer, hold their performance, and stay compliant, which lowers the true total cost of ownership over the life of the installation. An AMC turns unpredictable failure costs into a predictable line item — and keeps the asset doing its job.

Frequently asked

What is an Annual Maintenance Contract?

An AMC is an ongoing agreement to keep installed systems running — scheduled preventive maintenance, defined response times for faults, and often monitoring, spares and on-site support — for a fixed annual cost instead of paying per emergency.

Isn't it cheaper to just fix things when they break?

Rarely. Reactive repair brings unplanned downtime, emergency rates and premature equipment failure, which usually cost more than planned maintenance — and for fire and life-safety systems, scheduled testing is a compliance requirement, not a choice.

What is a response-time SLA?

It is a committed maximum time to respond to a fault, agreed in the contract and usually tiered by severity, so critical failures get the fastest attention. It turns 'we'll get to it' into a measurable, accountable service level.
